The process

From uncertainty to a documented next step.

The process is designed to separate facts from assumptions before anyone makes a proposal to a creditor.

01 · INITIAL ASSESSMENT

Tell us what is happening

What is owed, what is in arrears, who is contacting you and what outcome you are trying to reach.

02 · DOCUMENT CHECK

Build the file

We identify statements, notices, correspondence and payment records needed to understand the position.

03 · ROUTE CHECK

Confirm mediation is suitable

If the matter requires debt counselling, court representation or another regulated service, that distinction is made before negotiation begins.

04 · PROPOSAL

Prepare a realistic position

We help structure the numbers and terms the consumer can actually sustain.

05 · NEGOTIATION

Engage the relevant party

The proposal or dispute is communicated through the appropriate channel and responses are tracked.

06 · OUTCOME

Document and follow through

If an arrangement is reached, the terms should be clear. If it is not, we explain the available next routes.

What to prepare

A clearer file usually means a clearer conversation.

Useful documents

  • South African ID or identification document
  • Recent creditor statements
  • Collection letters or Section 129 notices
  • Summons or court documents, if any
  • Proof of payments already made
  • Recent income and expense information

Useful context

  • What changed financially
  • What you can realistically afford
  • Any prior arrangements
  • What you dispute and why
  • Deadlines or court dates
  • Your preferred outcome
Urgent legal deadlines matter. If you have been served with court documents, a notice of set-down, repossession-related papers or another time-sensitive legal document, mediation should not be treated as a substitute for urgent legal advice.
